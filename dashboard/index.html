Can the probabilities be trusted?

For every country, each model was refitted without its last 12 months, and we counted how many of those months fell inside the 80% prediction interval. A well-calibrated model lands near 80%. Below 80% means its intervals are too narrow; above means they are too cautious.

Method

Data: Ember monthly and yearly electricity data (CC-BY-4.0). Generation is modelled on the log scale, where solar's growing seasonal swing becomes additive.

Model selection has two stages. ADF and KPSS tests decide which differencing schemes give a stationary series. Within each accepted scheme, AIC picks the SARIMA lags. Rolling-origin cross-validation then picks between schemes, because AIC cannot compare models with different differencing.

Crossover: SARIMA is fitted to log(solar ÷ coal), 2,000 future paths are simulated, and the first year each path's annual mean rises above zero is recorded. The year probabilities assume recent trends continue; policy, prices or a new coal pipeline could bend them.
